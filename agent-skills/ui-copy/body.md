## Task

Read `references/copywriting.md` before writing anything. Then read the copy or screen the user supplied and establish three things before drafting:

1. **The silent question**: why should the reader care, right now? What is in it for them?
2. **The action**: what exactly happens when they act, in a specific verb and concrete outcome?
3. **The feedforward**: what happens next, and is anything risky or reversible?

If the user gave a screen rather than a line, inventory every string on it (headline, instructions, labels, CTAs, helper text, links, errors) and decide for each: keep, shorten, rewrite, or remove. Removal is a valid rewrite.

Write the rewrite, then run the checks:
- Read it aloud. If you would naturally change a word, change it.
- Copy Swap Test: strip logos and visuals; could a competitor paste these words unchanged? If yes, be more specific.
- Hesitation check: is there an unanswered doubt at the moment of action? If so, add one short reassurance line.

Do not invent product facts (limits, prices, guarantees). Where a fact is needed to be specific, mark it as a placeholder for the user to fill.

## Output format

### Before → after

| Element | Before | After | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. CTA | Tidy up | Review 4 quiet channels | Vague verb → specific action; adds feedforward |

One row per string. "What changed" names the mistake fixed (long, generic, duplicate, missing feedforward, no benefit) or the tip applied (benefit first, reassure, specific verb, real-person voice). Removed strings get "removed: <reason>".

### Variants

For the primary headline or CTA, give one to three alternatives with a one-line note on when each fits (tone, risk level, audience). There is more than one good answer; the fundamentals are what matter.

### Checks

- Copy Swap Test: pass or fail, with the line that would fail.
- Reassurance: where it was added and why, or "not needed".
- Open facts: placeholders the user must fill.

## Quality bar

- Every line has a specific verb and a concrete outcome; "Submit", "OK", "Continue" only when nothing more specific is true
- The benefit is visible within a second or two, without reading a paragraph
- No block of three or more lines survives unless it is legally required
- Nothing is said twice; nothing is said that the reader does not need at this moment
- Actions say what they do before they are taken; destructive or ambiguous outcomes are named, not hidden
- The voice is a person talking to a person, not a system announcing a state
- Rewrites preserve meaning and constraints; nothing is promised that the product does not do

## Anti-patterns

- System voice: "User validation is required because…" instead of "We sent a code to your phone"
- Explaining the mechanism instead of the benefit
- Generic praise or marketing fluff that a competitor could reuse
- A vague verb with an unknown consequence ("Clean up", "Optimize", "Manage")
- Reassurance that is itself vague ("Don't worry") rather than concrete ("You can change this later")
- Repeating the headline in the body, or the body in the helper text
- Rewriting for cleverness at the cost of clarity
