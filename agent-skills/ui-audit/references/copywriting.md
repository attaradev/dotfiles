# Copywriting pillar

Copy answers one question: **why should I care, right now?** Then it says what to do and what will happen next (feedforward).

## Three mistakes to look for

| Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Too long** | A block of three or more lines; people glance, skim, or leave. | Cut until only the necessary words remain. Short copy takes many drafts; that is normal. |
| **Too generic** | Words that leave the reader guessing or uninterested. | Name the specific benefit and the specific outcome. |
| **Unnecessary and duplicated** | Extra sentences or the same fact stated twice. | Remove; repetition hides what matters and raises the chance of confusion. |

## Four practical tips

1. **What's in it for me?** Before shipping a headline or CTA, make the benefit obvious within a second or two.
2. **Reassure.** People hesitate when something is uncertain. A line like "You can change this later" or "No prep needed" unblocks them.
3. **Use specific, action words.** Clear verbs and concrete outcomes; no guessing about what a button does.
4. **Talk like a real person.** Read it aloud. If you naturally change words while reading, the copy is off.

## The Copy Swap Test

Remove all logos and visuals and read the copy aloud. Could a competitor use the same words on their site? If yes, the copy is too vague.

## Feedforward

Every action should say what it will do before it is taken. A vague verb ("Tidy up", "Clean them up") with an unknown consequence (leave? archive? delete?) feels risky and gets skipped. Name the benefit, make the next step less scary (review before commit), and reduce the fear of acting.

## What a 5 looks like on an OTP screen

Before: a formal, programmatic paragraph explaining that validation is required because the workstation is unregistered, with the phone number and time limit buried in fine print.
After: one short line saying a code was sent to the user's phone, one instruction to enter it, and the time limit visible. Everything else removed.
