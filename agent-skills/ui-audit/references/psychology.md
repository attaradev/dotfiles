# Psychology behind C.L.E.A.R.

Use these to explain why a fix works. Each entry: effect, one-line design implication, source.

| Effect | Design implication | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Aesthetic-usability effect** | Polished, coherent screens are perceived as easier to use and minor issues are forgiven. Clarity work also makes the UI more beautiful. | Nielsen Norman Group, aesthetic-usability effect. |
| **Gestalt grouping** | People perceive structure (proximity, similarity, common region, continuity) before they read, then assign meaning. | Wagemans et al. (2012), *A Century of Gestalt Psychology in Visual Perception I*; Rosenholtz et al. (2009), *An Intuitive Model of Perceptual Grouping for HCI Design*. |
| **Cognitive load** | Working memory is limited; every visual mismatch costs re-interpretation. Reduce elements, patterns, and clutter. | Rosenholtz et al. (CHI 2005), *Feature Congestion: A Measure of Display Clutter*. |
| **Saliency** | Attention is pulled first by size, contrast, position, and spacing; this is what the Foggy Glasses Test approximates. | Itti, Koch and Niebur (1998), *A Model of Saliency-Based Visual Attention for Rapid Scene Analysis*; Agrawala, Stanford CS448B perception lecture. |
| **Von Restorff (isolation) effect** | An element that differs noticeably from its neighbours is spotted faster and remembered better. Make one thing different. | Von Restorff (1933). |
| **Reactance** | Overly forceful interfaces trigger pushback. Avoid extremes; offer a clear best next step and breathing room. | Rains (2013), reactance meta-analysis. |
| **Social presence** | Human cues (face, name) make an interaction feel like a person, not a record, and raise attention. | Emphasis lesson, customer-profile practice. |
| **Retrieval cues** | A second anchor (location, flag) gives memory more hooks; people recognise the record later. | Emphasis lesson, customer-profile practice. |
| **Error prevention** | Most failures are slips. Remove impossible choices, offer undo, confirm irreversible actions. | Nielsen, usability heuristic 5. |
| **Self-determination theory** | Motivation rests on autonomy/control, competence, and relatedness/recognition; the Reward Trifecta maps these to UI moments. | Deci and Ryan (2000); Bandura (1977) on self-efficacy; Baumeister and Leary (1995) on belonging; Gouldner (1960) on reciprocity; Izuma et al. (2008) on social reward. |
| **Goal-gradient effect** | Effort increases as the goal nears, and perceived (even illusory) progress raises completion and retention. | Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng (2006), *The Goal-Gradient Hypothesis Resurrected*. |
| **Goal setting** | Clear, specific goals with feedback drive performance; progress indicators and milestones apply this. | Locke and Latham (2002). |

Source course: Growth.Design, *The Psychology of UI Design* (C.L.E.A.R. framework). Further reading named in the course: *Refactoring UI* (Schoger), *Practical UI* (Dannaway), *Microinteractions* (Saffer), *Emotional Design* (Norman), *Designing for Emotion* (Walter), *Ogilvy on Advertising*, *Microcopy: The Complete Guide* (Yifrah).
