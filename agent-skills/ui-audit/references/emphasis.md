# Emphasis pillar

Layout makes a screen understandable. Emphasis makes its purpose unmissable. Hierarchy is relative: one element stands out only if the others step back.

## The Foggy Glasses Test (Squint Test)

Blur, zoom out, or squint at the screen. What remains is what the brain detects first: size, contrast, position, spacing. The most important element should still be obvious. If everything looks equally loud, or equally quiet, emphasis is not doing its job.

## Six emphasis dials

Dials, not switches: each can be turned up or down, and the best hierarchy usually comes from a few small adjustments, not every dial at maximum.

| Dial | Effect | Use it by |
|---|---|---|
| **Size** | Bigger reads as more important and is noticed first. | Make the primary message or action noticeably larger than its neighbours; turn down the surroundings so the hero does not carry all the work. |
| **Color** | Pulls attention, carries meaning, or tones things down. | Reserve the accent for key actions; use colour for status, warnings, success; mute secondary details. |
| **Space** | Whitespace isolates what matters. | Add breathing room around the primary element; buy space by shrinking or removing extras; keep related items close while isolating the group. |
| **Placement** | Early in the scan path gets noticed faster. | Put the primary message in prime real estate (top, first scan); place the action next to the information that justifies it. |
| **Visualization** | Show, don't tell. | Turn abstract text into a recognisable visual (progress bar, chart, icon, illustration); prefer at-a-glance meaning over paragraphs. |
| **Motion** | Movement grabs attention and shows change over time. | Use motion to guide attention, confirm change, or show progress; keep it subtle; no eye candy. |

## Three mistakes to look for

| Mistake | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| **Wrong dial** | Solving with the wrong lever (changing colour when the issue is placement or space). | Diagnose what is competing for attention first, then pick the dial that changes attention in that context; often placement and space before colour. |
| **Weak dial** | Everything adjusted a little, nothing stands out. | Fewer, bolder changes; one dial clearly different, visible at a glance. |
| **Screaming dial** | Several dials at maximum (giant, neon, heavy shadow, animated). Feels aggressive. | Establish a calm baseline, lead with one or two dials, let the rest support. Most hierarchy comes from what you turn down. |

If everything shouts, nothing stands out, and forceful interfaces make people push back (reactance). Give a clear best next step and enough breathing room to feel in control.

## Human-first emphasis

When a record represents a person, put photo and full name first (social presence raises attention), add a real-world anchor such as city and country (a second memory hook), and push IDs and admin links into the background.

## Worked example (OTP screen)

After layout, the screen was tidy but flat: small headline, a quiet main action that looked almost disabled, a loud but empty image area. Fix: scale up headline, instruction, field, and button (Size); centre the task panel with room around it (Space); strongest colour on the primary CTA, muted secondary links (Color); button directly under the field, full width, so instruction → input → action flows (Placement); an image that says "code on your phone" (Visualization). The CTA stays visually primary even before the code is typed: that is feedforward and is more accessible than a faint disabled state.
